3.  Train Pluse and Tempo

Focus on pulse, not just counting: Don't rely solely on counting "one, two, three, five, six, seven." Instead, train your ability to sustain a steady beat (pulse) underneath the music, as this is the true foundation of stability (

1.  **Practice with a metronome:** Use a metronome to train your consistency. Clap on beat
    
2.  **Incorporate vocalization:** Use your voice to "sing" the beats (e.g., "ta, da").
    
3.  **Train syncopation:** Once you are comfortable with the pulse, try clapping or marking the offbeats (the space *in between* the main beats). This trains your brain to feel the full structure of the music, not just the primary hits (3:43-4:15).
    

**Daily Excerise **

1.  1.  **Warm-up (2 mins):** Set a metronome to a slow, comfortable tempo. Relax your shoulders and just **clap on every beat**. Focus on consistency and staying perfectly in sync (1:36-2:33).
        
    2.  **Vocalization (3 mins):** Instead of clapping, use your voice to "sing" the beats (e.g., "ta, ta, ta"). This connects your breath and nervous system to the rhythm (3:00-3:40).
        
    3.  **Syncopation Training (3 mins):** Keep the beat with your voice, but clap on the **offbeats** (the silence *between* the pulses). This trains your brain to feel the complete structure of the music (3:43-4:40).
        
    4.  **Musical Application (2 mins):** Put on a real salsa track. Start by finding the pulse with your feet or a soft tap. Once grounded, add your vocalization and offbeat claps to the actual song
        

IMPORTANT Instruments of Salsa:

1.  **Conga Tumbao (2:20-2:36):**  Acts as an essential anchor. Its tones and slaps often align with the weight shifts and break steps in your salsa dancing.
2.  **Bass Tumbao (2:37-2:55):**  Provides the low-end frequency and the groove's foundation. It is often played  *contra tempo*  (off the main beats). Tuning into this helps you ride the groove rather than chasing the rhythm.
3.  **Piano Montuno (2:56-3:19):**  A repeating, syncopated loop that outlines the song's harmony. It locks in with the  *clave*  and helps you identify the cyclical structure and phrasing of the composition.
